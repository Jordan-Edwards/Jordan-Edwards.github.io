S-1 / J-6 Songbook

Roland AIRA Compact

Eight songs arranged for the J-6 Chord Synthesizer and the S-1 Tweak Synth. Every one has a J-6 setup, an S-1 patch, a full step grid to punch in by hand, and a MIDI file. Plug both in over USB and this page will play them straight into the gear.

MIDI not connected — you can still preview everything through your speakers.
How to get these onto the gear

Fastest — play them straight in over USB

  1. USB-C from the J-6 and the S-1 into your computer. Both are class-compliant, so there are no drivers to install.
  2. Open this page in Chrome or Edge (Web MIDI doesn't work in Safari or Firefox).
  3. Hit Connect MIDI, then pick each device in the two dropdowns.
  4. Pick a song and press Play. The J-6 gets the chords, the S-1 gets the bass or lead.
Each device shows up as its own USB MIDI port, so they can both stay on their default MIDI channel 1 — nothing to reconfigure. The page just sends to the right port.

To make it live on the S-1 by itself

  1. On the S-1 pick an empty pattern and set last step to the step count shown on the song.
  2. Dial in the patch from the S-1 Patch panel — the knob positions are written as clock faces.
  3. Punch the step grid in. Red steps are accents, blue ◠ means slide the step into the next one, and a dash is a rest.
  4. Set the tempo and hit play. Now it runs standalone with nothing plugged in.
On drive mode: AIRA Compacts do mount as a USB drive — power the unit off, hold PLAY, power it back on, and it appears as an external disk with BACKUP and RESTORE folders. That's Roland's own backup format for moving patterns you already made between a unit and your computer. The layout of those files isn't publicly documented, so it isn't a drop-box for arbitrary new patterns — these songs can't simply be copied in. Punching the grid in is still the way to get them resident on the S-1.

Or drag the MIDI file into a DAW

  1. Each song's Download .mid gives you a 3-track file: tempo, J-6 on channel 1, S-1 on channel 2.
  2. Route the two tracks to the two USB ports and the DAW drives both synths.
