Tracker Mini · looper · concept 3

Four loops you can read at arm's length

Up and Down pick a loop. The four keys under the screen act on it. Changes that would break the groove wait for the next bar, and the screen tells you so.

On a phone, turn it sideways to read the screen.

Press PLAY or the middle key. Sound starts with that press.

Key map

▲ ▼Pick a loop. The picked loop opens up; the others stay readable.
Fn1Direction: forward, reverse, back and forth. Changes at once and keeps the beat.
Fn2 · ◀ ▶Speed: ½, 1, 2. Changes at once and keeps the beat.
Fn3Mute. Waits for the next bar.
Fn4Record one pass of the loop, starting on the next bar.
Shift + FnRestart on the bar, solo, clear, and record again.
● / PLAYStart or stop everything.

How the screen reads

Each loop shows what it has played since it last turned round. Forward fills from the left, reverse from the right, and back and forth fills one way and then the other. You can tell the direction from across a room without reading anything.

Amber means "this is what you are working on" and nothing else. Red means recording. Grey is the audio. A muted loop drops to an outline.

The left column of every lane always says the same four things in the same place: number, state, where it is in bars, and anything waiting for the bar line.

Decisions

No glow, no gradients, no shadows. On a small screen in a dark room they cost contrast and the display budget, and they say nothing.

Up and Down pick lanes because lanes are stacked. The Fn keys sit under the screen, so their labels sit at the bottom of the screen right above them.

Mute and record wait for the bar because a mute on the wrong sixteenth sounds like a mistake. Direction and speed change at once because they keep the loop's phase.

On the device

Everything here is flat filled shapes, straight lines, one clip rectangle per lane and two typefaces' worth of glyphs: the cheapest things the Mini's display chip draws. Four stereo outlines at 96 points a side are roughly 3 KB of the 8 KB frame budget, by estimate.

A design mock in a web page with four short loops made in code. Not firmware; nothing here has run on a Mini. The key positions are a sketch.
