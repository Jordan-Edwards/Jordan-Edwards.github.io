Tracker Mini groovebox / direction: Handheld

Every screen is a little machine

Four screens at the real 800 x 480. Tap the keys under the screen. Menu steps through the screens. Turn the phone sideways to see it larger.

Tap a key. Shift latches here: tap once to hold it, again to let go.

What this is going for

The Mini is held like a console, so each mode is one object you can read at arm's length.

Loops is a tape between two reels. Perform is a string of lanterns. Capture is a jar that fills.

Every drawing is a meter or a control. The reels show where the loop is, the flame is the track level, the jar is the loop length.

Each direction has its own motion: forward fills left to right, reverse eats right to left, ping-pong is a ball between two bumpers.

Easy or hard on this screen

Easy: everything here is rectangles, rounded boxes, circles, lines and the five stock fonts. The display chip draws those cheaply. The big digits are built from rectangles, so they need no new font.

Hard: the waveform. 600 columns would use about 4.8 KB of the 8 KB display list, so the big lane is 96 chunky bars and the small lanes are 32. The look is also the budget.

Hard: smooth motion. There is no animation engine and the playhead moves in whole pixels, so every moving part is a small control re-queued each frame.

This is a concept picture drawn in a browser. It is not firmware and nothing here has run on the device. Where the keys sit on the case was not found in the source, so the layout under the screen is a guess.
